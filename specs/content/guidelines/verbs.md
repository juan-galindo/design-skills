## Gerund

A gerund is a verb form that ends in *-ing* in English, *-ando* or *-iendo* in Spanish, and *-ando* or *-endo* in Portuguese.

Avoid gerunds as the main action in a title or header for SP-all and PT-BR. Use the infinitive form or a noun instead.

| Region | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| SP-all | Do: Cómo crear tu cuenta en Bitso | Don't: Creando tu cuenta en Bitso |
| PT-BR | Do: Como criar sua conta na Bitso | Don't: Criando sua conta na Bitso |
| EN | Do: Creating your Bitso account · Do: How to create your Bitso account | |

---

Avoid gerunds to describe something that happened **after** the main action of the sentence, for SP-all and PT-BR.

| Region | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| SP-all | Do: Creaste tu cuenta y se generó una billetera automáticamente. | Don't: Creaste tu cuenta, generando una billetera automáticamente. |
| PT-BR | Do: Você criou sua conta e uma carteira foi gerada automaticamente. | Don't: Você criou sua conta, gerando uma carteira automaticamente. |
| EN | Do: You created your account and a wallet was generated automatically. · Do: You created your account, generating a wallet automatically. | |

## Future tense

The future tense is a verb form used to talk about actions that have not happened yet. Each language and region handles it differently.

Each region handles the future tense differently:

| Region | Rule | Do |
|---|---|---|
| SP-MX SP-CO | Conjugated future tense. | Do: Tus fondos estarán disponibles pronto. |
| SP-AR | Uses the "ir+infinitive" structure. The conjugated future tense can also be used in more formal contexts. | Do: Tus fondos van a estar disponibles pronto. · Do: Tus fondos estarán disponibles pronto. |
| PT-BR | Uses the "ir+infinitive" structure or the present tense. | Do: Seus fundos vão estar disponíveis em breve. · Do: Seus fundos ficam disponíveis em breve. |
| EN | Uses "will" + verb. Use the contracted form of "will" when the subject is a pronoun. | Do: Your funds will be available soon. · Do: You'll receive a confirmation email. |

## Contracted forms

Contractions are shortened forms of 2 words combined into one, using an apostrophe to replace the missing letters (e.g., *you are* → *you're*).

Use contractions in English when the subject is a pronoun, to keep the tone conversational and approachable.

| Region | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| EN only | Do: You'll receive a confirmation email. | Don't: You will receive a confirmation email. |

> **Note:** **Exception** — Avoid contractions when the subject is a noun.

| Region | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| EN only | Do: Bitso will send you a confirmation email. | Don't: Bitso'll send you a confirmation email. |

**Rationale** — Contractions with nouns sound unnatural and awkward in English. While grammatically possible, native speakers rarely use them in writing or speech. Contractions with pronouns (you'll, they'll) are universally accepted and feel natural, while contractions with nouns (Bitso'll, the app'll) can confuse readers and undermine Bitso's "confident yet approachable" tone.
