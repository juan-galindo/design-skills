## Thousands

Use a **comma** to separate thousands in Mexican and US markets, with no space between the comma and the numbers.

| Region | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| EN SP-MX | Do: 10,000 | Don't: 10.000 |

Use a **period** to separate thousands in Argentinian, Colombian and Brazilian markets, with no space between the period and the numbers.

| Region | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| SP-AR SP-CO PT-BR | Do: 10.000 | Don't: 10,000 |

---

## Decimals

Use a **period** to separate decimals in Mexican and US markets, with no space between the period and the numbers.

| Region | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| EN SP-MX | Do: 10,000.25 | Don't: 10.000,25 |

Use a **comma** to separate decimals in Argentinian, Colombian and Brazilian markets, with no space between the comma and the numbers.

| Region | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| SP-AR SP-CO PT-BR | Do: 10.000,25 | Don't: 10,000.25 |

---

Use 2 decimal places for **currency amounts**, whether the number is whole or has decimals.

| Region | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| EN SP-MX | Do: 12.40 | Don't: 12.4 |
| | Do: 12.00 | Don't: 12 |
| SP-AR SP-CO PT-BR | Do: 12,40 | Don't: 12,4 |
| | Do: 12,00 | Don't: 12 |

> **Note:** **Exception** — Show zero (0) with no decimals.

| Region | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| All | Do: 0 | Don't: 0,00 |

> **Note:** **Exception** — For percentages, use only as many decimal places as needed, but drop the last one if it's a zero.

| Region | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| EN SP-MX | Do: 12.4% | Don't: 12.40% |
| | Do: 12.45% | Don't: 12.450% |
| SP-AR SP-CO PT-BR | Do: 12,4% | Don't: 12,40% |
| | Do: 12,45% | Don't: 12,450% |

## Currency codes

### Fiat

Use the ISO code for all currencies after the number, with a space between the code and the number.

| Region | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| All | Do: 20 USD | Don't: 20USD |
| | | Don't: $20 |

> **Note:** The $ symbol applies to multiple currencies globally, which can create confusion in a multi-market platform like Bitso. For the same reason, we avoid other currency symbols like € or ¥. Using ISO codes (USD, MXN, BRL, ARS, COP) across all markets keeps amounts clear and unambiguous for every user.

> **Note:** **Exception** — We can use "pesos" instead of the ISO code in marketing campaigns targeting local audiences, where the word feels more natural and conversational.

Do: Puedes empezar con solo 100 pesos.

**Bitso's main fiat currency codes**

| Country | Currency | ISO code | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| United States | US dollar | USD | 25,000.50 USD |
| Mexico | Mexican peso | MXN | 25,000.50 MXN |
| Argentina | Argentine peso | ARS | 25.000,50 ARS |
| Colombia | Colombian peso | COP | 25.000,50 COP |
| Brazil | Brazilian real | BRL | 25.000,50 BRL |
