# Decision Alignment: Are we ready to evolve Product Design and Content Design into a unified Product Experience Builder role?

---

## Recommendation [Option A]

The design organization needs to increase the number of people who can drive product decisions from discovery to prototype with quality and speed. That is the real constraint on time to market today, not development capacity.

The recommended path evolves Content Design and Product Design toward a shared profile: the **Product Experience Builder**. This profile combines product thinking, excellent craft, and AI fluency. Content designers own the AI content systems that make scalable content production safe, and progressively take on product design work. Product designers build the AI foundations that allow others to design without a bottleneck. Squad composition shifts to increase the ratio of people who can make the right product decisions, reducing investment in roles that no longer represent the constraint.

This is not a cost reduction exercise. It is a deliberate reallocation toward the capabilities that drive product quality and time to market. Role changes are only justified if they move those metrics, and any transition that compromises quality or slows delivery must be caught and corrected.

**The ask is to approve a 6-month structured investment in 2 workstreams** to validate whether AI pipelines can safely automate content production and lower the barrier to product design. Results at month 6 will inform the decision on whether and how to scale this across the organization.

---

## 1. Context

### AI is reshaping what every role can do

- **AI is expanding every role's capability surface.** Regardless of current title, designers can now do more: strategize, create coded prototypes, write and ship faster when strategy and execution alignment are clear. The question are "what can Content Design role do?" "what should this role own?"
- **The AI era changes what makes a developer valuable in a squad.** Developers without a product mindset or design sensibility contribute less in a world where AI can generate implementation. What matters now is taste: the ability to judge what to build, not just how to build it. Squads carrying developers who don't meet that bar are over-invested in the wrong capability.

### The real bottleneck is product and design decision-making, not development

- **Development is not the bottleneck. Decision-making capacity is.** The constraint is the number of people with the product expereince taste and criteria to decide what is right problem to prototype. Squads need more people operating at that level, not more engineers coding.
- **Rebalancing squad ratios is the structural move.** Reducing the number of developers who don't have product mindset, and reinvesting that headcount into the areas that are now the real bottleneck (experience, decision-making, product thinking), is how squad capacity gets realigned to where value is actually created.

### Content Design cannot be eliminated yet

- **Content Design cannot be automated yet. The foundations don't exist.** Without clear principles, decision rules, and quality criteria, AI-generated content is inconsistent and off-brand. Content designers hold the expertise to build those foundations. Removing the role before that work is done creates a quality cliff.
- **Once foundations are in place, the nature of Content Design changes.** When content guidelines are well-defined and AI-ready, execution-level content work can be assisted or automated. The role shifts from writing copy to owning the system that produces it, and from there, toward broader product design contribution.

### The path forward

- **The model is bidirectional.** Content designers build the AI systems that make content production scalable. Product designers build the AI design foundations that allow content designers to build. Each role creates the conditions for the other to grow into the Product Experience Builder profile.
- **The highest-leverage move is growing content designers into Product Experience Builders.** They already have product taste and user empathy. Formalizing that into product design capability increases the pool of people who can drive a squad from discovery to prototype.

### The new archetype: Product Experience Builder

- **A new archetype is emerging in the industry: the Product Experience Builder.** This profile combines product thinking, excellent craft, and AI fluency. Craft here means the ability to produce work of high quality across disciplines: a prototype that feels right, a content system that holds under pressure, a design decision that is both fast and defensible. Product Experience Builders don't wait for a brief. They spot the right problem, decide what to prototype, and move from discovery to working artifact with the taste and execution quality to make it real.

## 2. Options

### 2.1 Option A: Evolve both roles toward the Product Experience Builder profile

Content Design takes on two scopes: own the AI content systems, and expand progressively into product design. Product Design builds the AI foundations that allow content designers to prototype without a product designer as the bottleneck. Squad composition shifts to increase the ratio of Product Experience Builders and reduce developers who lack product mindset.

**Arguments in favor**

- Grows the pool of people who can drive a squad from discovery to prototype, which is the actual constraint.
- Content designers are natural candidates: they already have product taste, user empathy, and a high bar for craft.
- Once content foundations are solid, production scales without proportional headcount growth.
- The talent pipeline compounds: every content designer who grows into the profile expands the senior product design pool.

**Arguments against**

- Owning two scopes simultaneously is a real cognitive load risk. Responsibilities must be sequenced, not stacked, and that requires active management.
- Not every content designer will want to evolve toward product design. Some chose this career for language and communication craft. Forcing the shift creates retention risk.
- Product designers absorbing content strategy and localization increases their execution time. The cognitive load problem applies in both directions.
- Squad configuration changes need Product and Engineering buy-in. Design cannot make this shift alone.

---

### 2.2 Option B: Keep roles as defined and monitor the market

Maintain current role definitions and revisit in 12 months.

**Arguments in favor**

- No disruption to ongoing work or team structure.
- Gives space to observe how other companies navigate this before committing.

**Arguments against**

- Passive positioning. Competitors who define their foundations now will have a head start.
- Leaves the question of what Content Design is for in an AI workflow unanswered. Ambiguity compounds.
- Tends to default to inertia without a clear trigger for action.

---

### 2.3 Option C: Consolidate Content Design into Product Design immediately

Eliminate Content Design and absorb its responsibilities into Product Design, assuming AI tooling covers the gap. This assumption is not valid today.

**Arguments in favor**

- Simplifies team structure.

**Arguments against**

- Eliminating the role does not eliminate the work. Content responsibilities move to product designers, which increases the design execution bottleneck rather than reducing it.
- Without content foundations in place, AI-generated content will be inconsistent. There is no one to define or enforce the quality bar.
- This is only viable after Option A succeeds and AI systems can absorb execution-level content work reliably.

---

## 3. Pilot: 2 workstreams, 6 months

This pilot is a time boxed effort to workflow validation before any team-wide change is made. Each workstream runs independently with a defined lead, a clear deliverable, and explicit success criteria. The General Manager of Retail, VP of Product, Product Lead, and Engineering Lead receive a monthly update, a summary at month three, and a full recommendation at month six, with a clear go, no-go decision for scaling.

---

### Workstream 1: AI content design pipeline - Shaping AI Design Maturity from L2 to L3

**Lead:** Juan Galindo (Investment)
**Team:** Marina (Savings) and Javier (Stocks).

Build and test the systems that allow content to be produced and maintained with AI assistance. The output is not just automated content; it is the foundations, decision rules, and quality criteria that make automation safe and consistent.

**Months 1 to 3: Foundations**

- Finish content guidelines v1: voice, tone, decision rules, and quality criteria tied to a real active surface
- First test of AI-assisted content using those guidelines; document what works and what breaks
- Identify which content surfaces are ready for automation and which still require human judgment.
- Build an AI-assisted pipeline for generating Lokalise keys and PT localization. 
- **Out of scope:** Full automation in production. This phase is definition and experimentation only.

**Months 4 to 6: Stress test**

- Run AI-assisted content on at least one live surface and measure quality against the guidelines
- Document where the pipeline holds and where it fails.
- Establish an AI-assisted pipeline for generating keys and full localization.
- Build an AI-assisted pipeline for creating content strategy, help center articles and asset bios.
- Retrospective: what does a content designer own once the pipeline is running?

**Tradeoffs**

- Nina will support savings only from a tactical perspective mainly generating keys, revieving localization and contributing to help center. She won’t be involved in product strategy or day to day workflows during the AI foundation build. UX content and localization will be created by Luiz our Savings Product Designer, based on content guidelines.
- If the pipeline is built but not maintained, it decays fast. Ownership after the pilot must be explicit before the pilot ends.

---

### Workstream 2: AI product design pipeline - Shaping AI Design Maturity from L2 to L3

**Lead:** Maria Magnani (Assets)
**Team:** Juan Galindo (Investment), Esmeralda Carreño (Journeys) and Manuel Cuevas (Adquisition)

Build and test AI-assisted prototyping and foundations that lower the barrier for a content designers and product managers to participate in product design without a product designer as the bottleneck.

**Months 1 to 3: Foundations**

- Map the current discovery-to-prototype process and identify where AI can reduce handoff friction
- Shape the first version of the design foundations that a non-product designer can use to prototype
- Test with at least one content designer: can they pick up a scoped design problem and build using what exists?
- **Out of scope:** Full autonomy for non-designers in production. This phase is tooling and enablement only.

**Months 4 to 6: Stress test**

- Run a full discovery-to-prototype cycle using the pipeline on a real squad problem
- Measure: did it reduce time to prototype, and did quality hold?
- Retrospective: what does a product designer own once the pipeline is running?

**Tradeoffs**

- Building design foundations for others to use takes product designers away from their own squad delivery. The short-term cost is real.
- If the foundations are not adopted after the pilot, the investment produces documentation, not capability.

---

### Pilot governance

- No title changes during the pilot. This is a scope and tooling experiment, not a promotion or demotion.
- Juan Galindo holds a biweekly sync between both workstream to share learnings across pipelines.
- Results from both workstreams reviewed together at month 6 before any structural decision is made.

### Recommendation wording for the pilot

When communicating this pilot to the team, avoid framing it as a role change or an organizational restructure. The recommended framing is:

> "We are running a 6-month experiment in two workstreams to understand how AI can help us work better. The goal is to learn what works and what doesn't before we make any team-wide decisions. Nothing changes in your role title during this period."

Key principles for how leads should talk about this:

- Lead with the business problem, not the role change: the bottleneck is in decision-making capacity, and we are testing whether AI pipelines can help.
- Be honest that this is an experiment. We do not know the outcome yet, and that is the point.
- Avoid language that implies inevitability ("the future of design") or threat ("roles that don't adapt will disappear"). Both create anxiety without adding clarity.
- When asked about what happens after the pilot, the honest answer is: we will review what we learned and decide together.

### Success signals

- **Time to market:** Discovery-to-prototype cycles are shorter with Product Experience Builder role, measurable as time from problem identified to testable artifact.
- **Product quality maintained:** Design and content quality scores do not drop as responsibilities shift. A regression here signals the transition is moving too fast.
- **Content foundations usable:** A Product Designer and Product Manager can use the content guidelines to prompt AI and produce on-brand output without a content designer reviewing every string.
- **AI output consistency:** AI-generated content reviewed against foundations passes quality bar at least 80% of the time without manual correction.
- **Product designer scope expansion:** At least one product designer owns a full discovery-to-prototype cycle with no hand-off gaps.

## 4. What should we learn from this decision?

### Operational learning

- Can content foundations be defined well enough for AI to use them reliably, or is the tacit knowledge too hard to codify?
- Does AI-assisted content with strong foundations reduce the volume of content reviews without sacrificing quality?
- Can a product designer own discovery-to-prototype without creating bottlenecks or quality drops in execution?
- Does enabling others to design create real leverage, or does it increase coordination overhead?
- When product designers absorb content strategy and localization, does execution time increase proportionally? At what point does that become unsustainable?
- When Product Experience Builder ratio increases, does overall squad output improve, stay the same, or regress? What is the right rebalancing point?

### Individual and role learning

- Can content designers shift from execution to AI foundation ownership without losing motivation or scope clarity?
- Does the Product Experience Builder profile feel like growth or like being stretched in incompatible directions?
- What level of AI fluency is actually required, and do current team members have it, or does it need to be developed?
